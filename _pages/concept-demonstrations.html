Waves through a locally resonant metamaterial

A chain of twelve unit cells sits between two non-dispersive media. Choose a frequency and see whether vibration crosses the chain, and how fast its phase and energy travel.

Formulation

This follows the negative mass (NM) monocoupled system and the method of Bhatt, Banerjee and Adhikari (2022), obtained from its generalised system by setting \(m_a=0\) and \(m_2=0\).

1. Unit cell and dynamic stiffness

Main-chain masses \(M\) at nodes 1 and 8 are joined by a spring \(K\). Each carries a resonator \(m_1\) (nodes 2 and 7) through a spring \(k_1\). The equations of motion are

\[ M\ddot{u}_1+K(u_1-u_8)+k_1(u_1-u_2)=0, \qquad m_1\ddot{u}_2+k_1(u_2-u_1)=0 \]
\[ M\ddot{u}_8+K(u_8-u_1)+k_1(u_8-u_7)=0, \qquad m_1\ddot{u}_7+k_1(u_7-u_8)=0 \]

With \(\ddot{(\bullet)}=-\omega^2(\bullet)\), condensing out nodes 2 and 7 gives the dynamic stiffness matrix, and its inverse gives the receptance matrix:

\[ \mathbf{D}_{yc}=\begin{bmatrix} D & -K\\ -K & D\end{bmatrix}, \qquad D=-\omega^2M+K+k_1+\frac{k_1^2}{\omega^2m_1-k_1} \]
\[ \boldsymbol{\alpha}_R=\mathbf{D}_{yc}^{-1}, \qquad \alpha_{LL}=\frac{D}{|\mathbf{D}_{yc}|}, \qquad \alpha_{LR}=\frac{K}{|\mathbf{D}_{yc}|} \]

2. Dispersion relation

Bloch's theorem relates the left and right displacements and forces through the propagation constant \(\kappa=\alpha+i\beta\):

\[ d_R=e^{i\kappa}d_L, \qquad f_R=-e^{i\kappa}f_L, \qquad \cos\kappa=\frac{\alpha_{LL}}{\alpha_{LR}}=g(\omega^2)=x \]

The wave propagates with \(\alpha=\cos^{-1}x\) and \(\beta=0\) when \(x^2\lt 1\). When \(x^2\gt 1\) it attenuates, with \(\alpha=\pi\) for \(x\lt -1\), \(\alpha=0\) for \(x\gt 1\), and \(\beta=\cosh^{-1}|x|\).

3. Peak and bounds of the attenuation band

Using \(\eta=\omega/\sqrt{K/M}\), \(\theta_{r1}=m_1/M\) and \(\eta_{r1}=\sqrt{k_1/m_1}/\sqrt{K/M}\), the dispersion relation takes the rational form

\[ g(\eta^2)=1+\frac{R(\eta^2)}{Q(\eta^2)}, \qquad R=\eta^4-\eta^2\eta_{r1}^2(1+\theta_{r1}), \qquad Q=\eta_{r1}^2-\eta^2 \]

The attenuation peak is at \(Q=0\), and the band boundaries are at \(R=0\) (\(\kappa=0\)) and \(R+2Q=0\) (\(\kappa=\pi\), the Bragg condition):

\[ \xi_{NM}=\eta_{r1}, \qquad \zeta_{r1}=\eta_{r1}\sqrt{1+\theta_{r1}} \]
\[ \eta_{B}^2=\frac{1}{2}\left[b\mp\sqrt{b^2-8\eta_{r1}^2}\right], \qquad b=2+\eta_{r1}^2(1+\theta_{r1}) \]

The demonstration uses \(\theta_{r1}=0.5\) and \(\eta_{r1}=1\). The Bragg boundaries are then \(\eta_B=0.848\) and \(1.668\), the peak is at \(\eta=1\), and \(\zeta_{r1}=1.225\).

Velocity formulation

In a propagation band, with \(L\) the cell length, the velocities in units of \(L\sqrt{K/M}\) follow from \(\cos\alpha=g(\eta^2)\):

\[ c_p=\frac{\eta}{\alpha}, \qquad c_g=\frac{d\eta}{d\alpha}=-\frac{\sin\alpha}{dg/d\eta} \]

\(\sin\alpha\) vanishes at both \(\kappa=0\) and \(\kappa=\pi\), so \(c_g=0\) at every band boundary, while \(c_p\) stays finite at the Bragg boundary. Solving \(\cos\kappa=g(\eta^2)\) gives each band exactly, with the minus sign for the lower band and the plus sign for the upper band:

\[ \eta^2(\kappa)=\frac{1}{2}\left[B\mp\sqrt{B^2-4(1-\cos\kappa)\,\eta_{r1}^2}\right], \qquad B=\eta_{r1}^2(1+\theta_{r1})+1-\cos\kappa \]

The wave packet panel is an exact sum of Bloch waves with a Gaussian spectrum of width \(\sigma_\kappa\), so it includes dispersive spreading:

\[ u(x,t)=\mathrm{Re}\sum_j e^{-(\kappa_j-\alpha)^2/2\sigma_\kappa^2}\,e^{i\left[\kappa_jx-\eta(\kappa_j)t\right]} \]

Impedance formulation

From the receptance relation and Bloch's theorem, with \(D=K\cos\kappa\), the force at the left node of the cell is

\[ f_L=\left(D-Ke^{i\kappa}\right)d_L=-iK\sin\kappa\;d_L \]

Dividing force by velocity amplitude gives the Bloch impedance of a propagating wave:

\[ Z_B=\frac{K\sin\alpha}{\omega}, \qquad \frac{Z_B}{\sqrt{KM}}=\frac{\sin\alpha}{\eta} \]

This value applies when the cell boundary passes through the node masses, as in this unit cell. It depends on where the boundary is placed. In the long-wave limit, it gives the impedance chosen for the two media:

\[ Z_0=\lim_{\omega\to0}Z_B=\sqrt{2K(M+m_1)}, \qquad c_0=\frac{\omega}{\kappa_0}=L\sqrt{\frac{K}{2(M+m_1)}} \]

Finite chain between the two media

The media are semi-infinite, so their far ends do not reflect. For incident, reflected and transmitted amplitudes \(U_I\), \(U_R\) and \(U_T\), with \(\kappa_0\) the wavenumber per cell and \(n\) measured from each interface:

\[ u_{\mathrm{left}}=\left(U_Ie^{i\kappa_0n}+U_Re^{-i\kappa_0n}\right)e^{-i\omega t}, \qquad u_{\mathrm{right}}=U_Te^{i\kappa_0n}e^{-i\omega t} \]

Assembling \(N=12\) cells gives a tridiagonal system for the node displacements \(d_0\) to \(d_N\):

\[ (D-i\omega Z_0)\,d_0-Kd_1=-2i\omega Z_0U_I \]
\[ -Kd_{n-1}+2Dd_n-Kd_{n+1}=0, \qquad 0\lt n\lt N \]
\[ -Kd_{N-1}+(D-i\omega Z_0)\,d_N=0 \]
\[ U_R=d_0-U_I, \qquad U_T=d_N, \qquad |U_R|^2+|U_T|^2=|U_I|^2 \]

In a propagation band this solves to the closed form

\[ \left|\frac{U_T}{U_I}\right|^2=\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{Z_0}{Z_B}-\frac{Z_B}{Z_0}\right)^2\sin^2(N\alpha)} \]

What happens at the band edges

\(Z_B\to0\) and \(c_g\to0\) at a band boundary. Taking this limit at \(\eta_B\) gives a transmission that is small but not zero, and falls as \(1/N\) instead of exponentially:

\[ \left|\frac{U_T}{U_I}\right|_{\eta_B}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\left(N\eta_BZ_0/2\sqrt{KM}\right)^2}} \]

For this chain that is 11% at \(\eta=0.848\) and 6% at \(\eta=1.668\). The infinite-chain band edge is sharp, but a finite chain has only a gradual transition around it. The impedance mismatch also reflects most of the wave just inside the band, so a finite chain is not a sharp switch at this frequency.
