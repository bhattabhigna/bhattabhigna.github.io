Waves through a locally resonant metamaterial

A chain of twelve mass-in-mass cells sits between two non-dispersive media. Choose a frequency and see whether vibration crosses the chain, and how fast its phase and energy travel.

Governing equations

1. Mass-in-mass chain

Each cell has an outer mass \(m_1\) with displacement \(u_n\), and an internal resonator \(m_2\) with displacement \(v_n\). The cells are joined by springs \(k_1\), the resonator is attached by \(k_2\), and the cell length is \(L\).

\[ m_1\ddot{u}_n = k_1\left(u_{n+1}-2u_n+u_{n-1}\right)+k_2\left(v_n-u_n\right), \qquad m_2\ddot{v}_n = k_2\left(u_n-v_n\right) \]

2. Effective mass

For harmonic motion at frequency \(\omega\), with resonator frequency \(\omega_0^2=k_2/m_2\), eliminating \(v_n\) gives a frequency-dependent mass. It becomes negative just above \(\omega_0\).

\[ v_n=\frac{\omega_0^2}{\omega_0^2-\omega^2}\,u_n, \qquad m_{\mathrm{eff}}(\omega)=m_1+\frac{m_2\,\omega_0^2}{\omega_0^2-\omega^2} \]

3. Dispersion relation and bandgaps

Substituting the Bloch solution \(u_n=U\,e^{i(\omega t-qnL)}\) gives

\[ \cos(qL)=\Gamma(\omega)=1-\frac{m_{\mathrm{eff}}(\omega)\,\omega^2}{2k_1} \]

Waves propagate when \(|\Gamma|\le 1\). Otherwise \(q\) is complex and the amplitude decays along the chain, which defines a bandgap:

\[ \mathrm{Im}(qL)=\cosh^{-1}|\Gamma(\omega)| \]

4. Phase and group velocity

The phase velocity is the speed of a crest, and the group velocity is the speed of the envelope. Differentiating the dispersion relation gives \(c_g\) directly.

\[ c_p=\frac{\omega}{q}, \qquad c_g=\frac{d\omega}{dq}=-\frac{L\sin(qL)}{d\Gamma/d\omega} \]

The wave packet is drawn using the first-order form, which neglects the packet spreading over time:

\[ u(x,t)\approx A\!\left(x-c_g t\right)\cos\!\left(qx-\omega t\right) \]

5. Finite chain between non-dispersive media

The two media are semi-infinite, so their far ends do not reflect. Their wave speed and impedance match the long-wave limit of the chain, with \(\kappa=\omega/c_0\):

\[ c_0=L\sqrt{\frac{k_1}{m_1+m_2}}, \qquad Z=\sqrt{k_1\left(m_1+m_2\right)} \]

With incident amplitude \(a\), reflected amplitude \(r\) and transmitted amplitude \(t\), and with \(x\) measured from the first cell on the left and from cell \(N\) on the right, the fields are

\[ u_{\mathrm{left}}=\left(a\,e^{-i\kappa x}+r\,e^{i\kappa x}\right)e^{i\omega t}, \qquad u_{\mathrm{right}}=t\,e^{-i\kappa x}\,e^{i\omega t} \]

Force balance at each cell gives a tridiagonal system for \(N=12\) cells:

\[ \left(k_1-\omega^2 m_{\mathrm{eff}}+i\omega Z\right)u_1-k_1u_2=2i\omega Z\,a \]
\[ -k_1u_{n-1}+\left(2k_1-\omega^2 m_{\mathrm{eff}}\right)u_n-k_1u_{n+1}=0, \qquad 1<n<N \]
\[ -k_1u_{N-1}+\left(k_1-\omega^2 m_{\mathrm{eff}}+i\omega Z\right)u_N=0 \]

The interface conditions then give \(r\) and \(t\). With no damping, energy is conserved:

\[ r=u_1-a, \qquad t=u_N, \qquad |r|^2+|t|^2=|a|^2 \]

Parameters used: \(m_2/m_1=0.5\) and \(k_1/m_1=\omega_0^2\). Frequencies are normalised by \(\omega_0\), and velocities are given in units of \(L\omega_0\).
