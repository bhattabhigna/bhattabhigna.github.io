Local resonance in phononic crystals

In an ordinary phononic crystal, bandgaps come from Bragg scattering, so they open only where the wavelength is comparable to the period. A locally resonant metamaterial adds an internal resonator to each cell, here a mass \(m\) on a spring \(k\). Near the resonator's natural frequency, the resonator moves out of phase with the main chain and pushes back on it, so the effective mass of the chain turns negative and waves can no longer propagate. This opens an attenuation band at the resonance frequency itself, which can be far below the Bragg limit, and lets a small structure block low-frequency vibration. The demonstration above uses the negative mass (NM) system from reference 1.
